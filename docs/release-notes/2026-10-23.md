# 2026-10-23

## Large sessions open and follow faster

### Board and CLI

- **Reopening a session is quick again after trocar was idle.** A session already read is reopened from small index
  files written beside its context file while trocar is idle, instead of rescanning that file. On a 16k-call live
  session the first answer after an idle restart went from about 1.1–1.8 s to about 0.28 s.
- **Catching up on a session that grew while trocar was idle is faster.** On the same live session, reading the new
  part after a restart took about 10 s instead of 1.5 to 4 minutes. While an agent keeps working, each new call adds
  about 3 KB (about 1 KB compressed) to the board's answers on a generated 6.6k-call session; following a lane costs
  about 5 ms per new call, while the session answer's work per new call is still above the design target.
- **The first open of a big session shows its progress moving and takes about half the time.** While trocar reads a
  session for the first time, its progress now moves from the start instead of sitting near 10 % until every call was
  read. On an 18.9k-call live session the first full read took about 6 minutes instead of about 12.
- **The replay desk stutters much less while a big session is read for the first time.** Each update used to
  redraw the rows of sub-agent calls by checking every call against every main step, and applied a thousand new
  calls in one go. The desk now looks only at the calls in view, applies a big update in short pieces, and draws the
  outline and side panels just after the conversation. On a 19.6k-call live session, jumping between lanes no longer
  freezes the page, and stepping with the arrow keys freezes it less than half as often. Some single steps through a
  very long conversation still take over 50 ms on a busy machine.
- **`trocar query session` on a session still being read waits for the new part only.** While the answer says
  `pending`, the CLI asks for what changed since its last answer, and prints one complete answer at the end.

## The board reads at a steady pace

### Board

- **The session list refreshes every 15 s, and right away when you come back to the tab.** The session library and
  the overview's recent list used to re-read every 3 s; a new session can now take up to 15 s to appear unless you
  return to the tab or press retry. Coming back to the tab, retrying or switching capture no longer reads the same
  thing twice in a row.
- **A failing read waits longer between tries.** On every screen, a read that keeps failing now waits twice as long
  after each failure, up to 30 s, instead of retrying every few seconds; the first good answer brings back the usual
  pace.

## A lane's files and resources come in pages

### CLI

- **A lane answer stays within 1 MB.** A lane's seen files and resources used to come in full beside every page, so a
  long lane could send several MB at once. They now fill what room the page leaves; the answer's `ledger_next` names
  the rest, and `trocar query lane --id <id> --lane <key> --ledger <ledger_next>` gives the next part, until
  `ledger_next` is null. `--ledger` cannot be given with `--from` or `--to`.

## The replay desk reads as you go

### Board

- **No more "Load this step".** The replay desk reads a session's lanes in pages as you move: a step whose page has
  not arrived keeps its outline, costs and cache cells on screen and fills in when the page comes; nothing on the
  desk asks you to load anything. While a large session is still being read, one quiet line under the session header
  says how far the read has got; it has no controls.
- **The resource explorer's counts are exact or blank.** A lane's files and resources can come in several parts; the
  explorer reads them all for the lane you are looking at before it shows a group's count, and leaves the count blank
  until then. "Files read" lists the files the lane read but did not open, however many there are.
- **"Question K" is the agent's own count.** The step head's question number comes from the session itself, and is
  left out where the part of the lane on hand cannot say it, instead of counting only the questions loaded so far.
- **A step's reply loads on a real board.** The desk asked for a step's reply text with a parameter the server does
  not take, so on a real board the reply of a step you stopped on could say it could not be read.

## Updates no longer pause capture for 30 s

### CLI and collector

- **`trocar update` replaces the collector as soon as its responses have finished.** The update waited the full 30 s
  every time, because it could not read the collector's count of connections still finishing; the agents that had
  capture on were not captured for that time, and whatever was still open after it was cut. It now goes on as soon as
  nothing is finishing; a response that takes longer still gets up to 30 s. `uninstall` takes the same path.
- **What an update or uninstall cut says so.** The connections cut when `update` or `uninstall` stopped the collector
  used to say "cut by trocar off --now", and the collector's stop record said `off`, though nobody ran `off`. They now
  say the connection was cut when `trocar update` (or `uninstall`) stopped the collector, and the stop record names that
  command. `trocar off --now` is labelled as before.

## Your own programs are not refused while the Mac sleeps

### Collector (macOS)

- **`status` Problems no longer lists your own apps as "a program not shown to be yours" after a night with the lid
  closed.** During the short wakes a closed Mac makes, the check of a connecting program's details could time out, and
  the door of a proxy settings file (PAC) then refused it, though trocar had already seen that the program is yours. Such
  a connection is now let through (forwarded, not captured), and the app gets the PAC with trocar in it. When a program
  is still refused, the record says why its details could not be read.

## The replay address follows what you see

### Board

- **Reloading a session you follow keeps following.** While "Follow latest" is on, the replay address names no step, so
  a reload, or the same link opened later, lands on the newest step and keeps following. Before, the address named the
  step on screen, so a reload stopped following at that step.
- **The address always names the step on screen.** A link with a step opens on that step with following off, even when
  it is the newest step. Stepping with the arrow keys writes the step into the address at once, so a reload right after
  a key press lands on the same step; quick runs of steps update the address at most about three times a second.
  Turning following off with its button now writes the step too, as the f key did.
- **Long sessions step smoothly.** A step whose conversation has a thousand or more blocks used to redraw every row;
  the replay desk now draws only the rows that changed and one screen around where you land, and fills in the rest in
  short pieces afterwards. On a 20k-call live session, a step with about 1,200 blocks is drawn in about 15 ms instead
  of about 27 ms, with no freeze.
